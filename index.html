Zhenlin An

I am an assistant professor in the School of Computing at the University of Georgia. I was a postdoctoral researcher at the University of Pittsburgh and Princeton University, advised by Dr. Longfei Shangguan and Prof. Kyle Jamieson. Prior to that, I was a postdoc and received my Ph.D. at The Hong Kong Polytechnic University, advised by Dr. Lei Yang and Prof. Jiannong Cao.

My research centers on mobile computing, wireless systems, and their intersection with AI. My long-term vision is to build intelligent networks and sensing systems that can understand the physical world, reason about radio and sensor signals, and adapt through interaction with real and simulated environments. Our recent focus is on agentic AI for wireless, IoT, and sensing systems, including radio digital twins, autonomous network simulation and optimization, and agents for interpreting IoT sensing data in health and daily life.

I am looking for multiple self-motivated PhD students and research assistants (Spring/Fall 2026). Feel free to reach out: an.zhenlin@outlook.com

Research Overview

My recent research is organized around the following directions (see Publications for the full list):

AI-Native Wireless and Radio Twins
Building AI-native wireless systems and radio twins that model, simulate, optimize, and reason about complex radio environments.

Building sensing systems that use acoustic, radio, and other signals to understand people, activities, and environments.

Ubiquitous IoT Network
Building ubiquitous ultra-low-power IoT systems that extend communication and sensing into concrete, water, the human body, and everyday environments.
